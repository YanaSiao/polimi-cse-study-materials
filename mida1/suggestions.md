# Suggestions & Personal Experience

## Who Should Take This Course?

There are mainly two types of people taking this course:
- those who to take it as a mandatory burden and find it incredibly boring (the absolute majority), 
- people like me, who find it relaxing, predictable, and well structured

This course is not considered an easy one. The theory is dense, and you need to learn each step of the procedures in detail.

There are no mandatory practical sessions, although I strongly recommend following the coding seminars if they are available. I followed seminars organized by professor Piroddi and held by a former student.

Since the course is semi-semester, you have to attend 8 hours per week, and I strongly encourage not skipping lessons, since they are strictly connected to each other.

On the other hand, you have the opportunity to take the exam in April. In that case, however, you must skip one of the June–July–September exam sessions.

You should take this course if you want to dive deeply into AI and understand the mathematics behind it.

If you only need to collect credits and cannot stand mathematics, statistics, and linear algebra, choose another course and do not make yourself suffer.


## Exam Structure

The exam consists of:
- around 20 multiple-choice questions with one correct answer
- 3–4 theoretical questions

The multiple-choice questions can usually be grouped into approximately three larger exercises.

- The first part is worth up to 21 points
- The second part is worth up to 12 points

Each multiple-choice question is worth between 0.5 and 2.0 points, while wrong answers carry a penalty between 0.1 and 0.5 points.

You have 80 minutes, which is barely enough to finish everything if you do not make mistakes and do not need to restart any exercise.

You must submit the test part through Google Forms.

While solving the exercises, you have to mark your answers on paper and later use those notes to fill in the form. The form contains only:
- the number of the question
- the options: A, B, C, D

The actual questions and answers are not shown in the form.

Do not mess this up — not all exam coordinators allow you to look again at your paper after you start filling in the form.


## How to Prepare for the Exam

This course requires solid knowledge of:
- linear algebra  
- calculus  
- probability and statistics  

Learning these topics on the spot is **not a wise strategy**, as the professors assume that you already covered these fundamentals during your Bachelor’s degree.

The theory questions have been repetitive over the last 5 years. Some topics appear much more frequently than others.

Be prepared to:
- reconstruct proofs
- explain theories step by step
- remember the logic of procedures

I recommend creating short chains of keywords that help you reconstruct the full explanation and memorize it.

The practical part of the test mimics the advanced exercises from the exercise sessions and mainly consists of:
- Stochastic Process evaluation
  - variance
  - covariance
  - spectral density
- Canonical Representation and Model Prediction
- Model Identification

My best approach was solving the advanced exercises without notes and making sure I truly understood the algorithms.

When preparing, train with a timer and aim to finish in about 70 minutes.


## Personal Opinion About the Course

I am one of the rare fans of MIDA.

It is a pity that the course misses a practical component and concentrates almost exclusively on theory. However, the theory is strongly connected to the foundations of AI, such as:
- loss functions
- algorithm complexity
- convergence
- bias&variance tradeoff

The unfortunate part is that I found it somewhat standalone, without a natural continuation or practical application. I wish there had been a project, a practical data analysis course, or something similar afterward.

I really appreciated the teaching style of professor Formentin. He first provides an overview with the key milestones that form a map of the course, and then moves from one milestone to the next. The rhythm of the course feels very homogeneous throughout the semester.

I tried attending exercise sessions with different professors, but I did not find any that matched my learning style. In the end, I stayed with Rapizza, whose sessions worked well for my exam preparation.
