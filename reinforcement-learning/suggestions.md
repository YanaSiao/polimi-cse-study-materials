# Subject Suggestions & Personal Experience

## Who Should Choose This Subject?

I believe most Master’s students **should not take this course**, as it does not match the expectations many people have.

This course is **highly theoretical** and is based on dense mathematical foundations:
- theorems  
- proofs  
- formal definitions  

Many students expect to gain **practical knowledge** that complements other AI courses. However, the practical sessions here are **optional**, do **not contribute to the final evaluation**, and feel more like additional material rather than the core of the course.

You might be motivated to take this course because of the **trendiness of the topic**, but the level of depth is **higher than any other Master’s course** I have taken or heard about.

Unfortunately, I found this course somewhat **standalone** — I did not encounter other courses that directly complement or apply the knowledge gained here.

If you are expecting a short **3-week preparation**, I should clarify expectations:  
you will likely need **as much time as for a 10 CFU Master’s course** to prepare properly.


### Who *should* take it

This course is perfect for someone who:
- wants to pursue a career in **theoretical research**, academia, or a **PhD**
- is interested in **deep understanding** rather than application
- is not afraid of a **serious intellectual challenge**

Before starting the course, I strongly recommend reviewing:
- calculus  
- linear algebra  
- probability&statistics  


## How to Prepare for the Exam

This course has gone through several editions with different evaluation formats.  
As more Master’s students enroll, professors are moving from a **pure oral exam** to **hybrid formats**.

### Exam structure (2026)

- **Written pre-evaluation test**
  - 10 questions  
  - 20 minutes  
  - You must score at least **9/10** to access the oral exam  
  - Does **not contribute** to the final grade  

- **Oral exam**
  - Immediately after the written test  
  - 3 rounds of questions (one per professor)  
  - Each professor can assign up to **11 points**

This means you **cannot compensate** weak knowledge in one area with strong performance in another — you need to be **consistently prepared across all topics**.

### Study Strategy

Bad news:
- You need to know **everything**
  - definitions  
  - properties  
  - theorems and proofs  
  - equations  
  - algorithms  
  - differences between methods  

Good news:
- It’s a no-brainer — just **learn everything**


## Personal Opinion on the Course

I appreciated this course because it gave me a real sense of the **depth of modern AI research**.

In many other courses, I often felt that we were studying **historical or simplified versions** of methods, rather than something applicable outside the university.  
In this RL course, I felt like I reached much closer to the **core of the field**.

That said, it was a **difficult journey**, and I spent significantly more time studying than I initially expected.


## Final Thoughts

Reinforcement Learning is a **dense and demanding course** that does not waste time and provides a **complete and rigorous picture** of RL algorithms and methods.

It is a great starting point if you are considering doing your **thesis in RL**.

The course is **top-tier in difficulty**, but not impossible.  
Be prepared to:
- work consistently  
- invest significant time  
- pay close attention to details
